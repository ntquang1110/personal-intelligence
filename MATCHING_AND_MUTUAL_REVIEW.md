# Matching & Mutual Review — Product Extension

## Why this exists
Personal Intelligence currently focuses on understanding one person. Matching adds a second question: given a specific opportunity or relationship, how well do the parties fit one another, what evidence supports that view, and what should they clarify together?

This is a decision-support workflow, not an automated gatekeeper, ranking authority, or substitute for human judgment.

## Three distinct modes
1. **Self-fit:** assess the person's fit for an opportunity or relationship against their own values, goals, strengths, constraints, and non-negotiables.
2. **Other-party review:** an employer, recruiter, or potential partner reviews a deliberately shared profile for a stated purpose.
3. **Mutual review:** both parties independently describe needs and expectations, then compare overlap, differences, unknowns, and topics to discuss.

Never treat a one-sided profile as a complete account of a relationship or as proof of another person's motives.

## A. Job and career matching
### Candidate-side questions
- Which outcomes has the person delivered, and what evidence demonstrates capability?
- Which responsibilities, work conditions, values, and growth paths are energizing or draining?
- What constraints, compensation range, location, schedule, and risk preferences are relevant and voluntarily shared?
- What gaps are learnable, and what support would help the person succeed?

### Employer-side questions
- What outcomes must the role deliver in the first 3, 6, and 12 months?
- Which skills are essential on day one versus trainable?
- What are the real work conditions, decision rights, manager expectations, and success criteria?
- What evidence can the candidate provide, and what evidence can the employer provide about the role?

### Output
A role-fit brief with evidence-backed strengths, requirements match, gaps, likely support needs, unknowns, questions for both sides, and a reversible next step such as a structured interview or work sample.

### Fairness guardrails
Do not infer or score protected or sensitive characteristics. Do not use health, disability, race, ethnicity, religion, sex, sexual orientation, age, family plans, marital status, or unrelated personal history as fit signals. Do not make hiring decisions automatically. Use job-related criteria, consistent questions, accessible processes, and human review. Avoid opaque composite scores; explain each finding and its evidence.

## B. Partner and marriage compatibility
### Explore mutually, not as a secret dossier
- Core values and what they mean in daily behavior.
- Desired relationship structure, commitment, timelines, and expectations.
- Children and parenting preferences, if relevant and voluntarily discussed.
- Family involvement, boundaries, caregiving, and living arrangements.
- Money: spending, saving, debt, risk, transparency, and responsibilities.
- Communication, disagreement, repair, emotional availability, and conflict patterns.
- Lifestyle, location, work ambitions, time together, friendships, and independence.
- Intimacy, affection, and boundaries only to the degree each person chooses to discuss.
- Deal-breakers, flexible preferences, open questions, and areas where each person is willing to negotiate.

### Output
A mutual compatibility map: shared priorities, complementary differences, material conflicts, unknowns, confidence, and conversation prompts. Do not produce a universal “spouse score” or label someone as good/bad spouse material. Compatibility is contextual, reciprocal, and can change over time.

### Safety guardrails
Both people should choose what to disclose. Never secretly profile a partner from private messages, accounts, or third-party data. Do not diagnose, predict abuse with certainty, or infer character from one-sided anecdotes. If there are threats, coercion, or fear, prioritize safety and trusted professional support over compatibility scoring.

## C. Mutual review protocol
1. Each party independently defines the purpose, needs, must-haves, flexible preferences, and questions.
2. Each person creates a purpose-specific shareable profile from evidence they approve.
3. Each party reviews the same agreed criteria; separate evidence from impressions.
4. Compare overlap, complementary strengths, conflicts, missing evidence, and assumptions.
5. Let each party correct factual errors and add context before synthesis.
6. Produce questions and possible experiments, not a verdict.
7. Each party independently chooses whether to proceed. Neither party owes disclosure or continuation.

## Match report format
- Purpose, scope, and date.
- Criteria agreed in advance.
- Evidence from each side and its source.
- Strong alignment and complementary fit.
- Potential friction and alternative explanations.
- Unknowns and confidence limits.
- Questions for both parties.
- Suggested next step and each person's independent choice.

## Non-goals
- No universal compatibility percentage.
- No hidden ranking of people.
- No automated hiring or relationship decisions.
- No public personal dossier.
- No treating a model-generated inference as verified fact.
