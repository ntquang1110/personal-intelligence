# Shared Matching Engine

## Purpose
Use one explainable comparison method for two different contexts: career fit (person ↔ role/employer) and relationship compatibility (person ↔ person). The engine compares needs and evidence; it does not rank human worth or decide outcomes.

## Inputs
- A clearly stated decision and context: `job`, `partner`, or `marriage`.
- Purpose-specific profiles that each owner approved for this exact use.
- Criteria agreed before examining the result.
- Evidence references, constraints, must-haves, preferences, unknowns, and review date.
- For mutual review, explicit voluntary participation from every participant.

## Shared vocabulary
For every criterion, use one status:
- `aligned`: available evidence indicates the needs/expectations are compatible.
- `complementary`: different preferences or capabilities may work well together, with a clear explanation.
- `unresolved`: evidence is missing, ambiguous, or not yet discussed.
- `conflicting`: current evidence indicates a meaningful incompatibility.
- `not_applicable`: criterion does not apply to this decision.

Evidence status for role requirements may additionally be `demonstrated`, `partial`, `not_yet_evidenced`, or `not_applicable`. Do not conflate missing evidence with inability.

## Workflow
1. **Frame:** state the decision, scope, date, and what the output will not decide.
2. **Consent & scope:** confirm each profile owner approved the exact fields and audience. If not, stop and request approval; do not synthesize private information.
3. **Define criteria first:** separate must-haves, flexible preferences, and useful-but-optional factors. Record who proposed each criterion.
4. **Normalize evidence:** map every claim to source, claim type, date/context, confidence rationale, and counterevidence when available.
5. **Compare symmetrically:** assess fit in both directions. For jobs, evaluate candidate-to-role and role-to-candidate; for partners, preserve each person's own stated needs rather than assuming one is the default.
6. **Identify friction and alternatives:** explain likely impact, conditions under which a difference is workable, and plausible alternative interpretations.
7. **Find the next evidence:** propose the smallest respectful test or conversation likely to resolve a high-impact unknown.
8. **Invite correction:** each participant reviews claims about themselves and can correct facts or add context before the report is finalized.
9. **Return agency:** show trade-offs and options; each party independently decides whether to proceed.

## Output contract
- Purpose, context, scope, and date.
- Criteria agreed in advance and their importance.
- Evidence on each side with provenance.
- Alignments, complementary differences, unresolved items, and conflicts.
- Impact, confidence rationale, alternative explanations, and unknowns.
- Questions / low-risk experiments for both parties.
- Corrections requested and limitations.
- Explicit statement: human decision required; no automatic rejection or relationship verdict.

## Decision rules
- Never generate a single opaque compatibility score or universal ranking.
- Do not average away a deal-breaker. Mark a stated must-have conflict explicitly and invite direct clarification.
- A high number of aligned low-importance preferences cannot cancel one material unresolved or conflicting must-have.
- Do not infer motives, diagnoses, protected characteristics, or personal history from gaps.
- For hiring, use only job-related criteria and a consistent process; do not use protected or sensitive traits as fit signals.
- For relationships, use voluntary direct disclosure; never build a secret dossier from private messages or accounts.
- Keep observations, self-reports, inferences, hypotheses, and unknowns distinct.
- When evidence is one-sided, label the report provisional and do not speak for the absent party.
- Never claim security, expiry, or revocation unless the runtime enforces it.

## When to stop
Stop if consent is absent, the requested comparison uses discriminatory or invasive criteria, the data source is unauthorized, or the process could create a misleading automated gatekeeping decision. For threats, coercion, or fear in a relationship context, pause compatibility analysis and prioritize safety.
