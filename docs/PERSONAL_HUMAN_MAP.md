# Personal Human Map (PHM)

## Product intent
Build an evolving, evidence-linked model of a person that explains how they think, decide, learn, act, relate to environments, and change over time. The primary objective is depth of mechanism, not assigning a personality label.

## Non-goals
- No single test or typology defines a person.
- No diagnosis of mental-health conditions.
- No inference of competence, destiny, intelligence, or suitability from date of birth, astrology, numerology, or typology alone.
- No unsupported composite score that hides disagreement or missing evidence.

## Evidence lanes
1. **Empirical lane**: interviews, structured assessments, cognitive/decision tasks, behavioral episodes, work samples, longitudinal outcomes, and authorized observer feedback.
2. **Interpretive/cultural lane**: astrology, numerology, zodiac and other symbolic traditions. Keep these outputs explicitly labeled as reflection prompts or cultural interpretations. Never merge their claims into empirical findings or confidence ratings.
3. **User meaning lane**: what the person says matters to them, including values, identity, and personal interpretations. Preserve as self-report, not objective fact.

## Human map domains
- Context and life history
- Personality traits and behavioral tendencies
- Cognitive process: framing, decomposition, evidence use, uncertainty, alternatives, decision rules, revision
- Motivation and values
- Capabilities: demonstrated, developing, and untested
- Behavioral patterns across situations
- Stress responses and recovery (non-clinical)
- Environment fit and collaboration
- Blind spots, contradictions, and rival explanations
- Development hypotheses and experiments

## Claim lifecycle
Each meaningful claim has a stable ID, evidence references, evidence independence, counterevidence, alternative explanations, scope conditions, confidence rationale, falsification/validation test, and review status. Statuses are not interchangeable:
- observed
- supported_inference
- hypothesis
- unknown
- contradicted
- retired

Evidence labels describe the source, not truth: self-report, observed behavior, work sample, standardized assessment, observer feedback, longitudinal outcome, or symbolic interpretation.

## Mechanism-first synthesis
Prefer the chain:
**trigger/context → attention and interpretation → reasoning/decision strategy → action → outcome → learning/update**.
Do not infer an internal mechanism from an outcome alone. Record competing mechanisms when the same behavior has multiple plausible causes.

## Capability rules
Distinguish:
- interest: the person wants to do it;
- knowledge: the person can explain it;
- skill: the person can perform it in a bounded task;
- demonstrated capability: performance is repeatable in relevant conditions;
- potential: a hypothesis supported by learning rate, transfer, and response to feedback, still requiring validation.

## Confidence
Use qualitative levels with rationale: low, moderate, high. Confidence depends on source quality, relevance, independence, recency, consistency, and counterevidence. Repetition from the same conversation or copied source is not independent corroboration. Do not calculate a numerical personality score unless the instrument has a defined, valid scoring method and interpretation.

## Longitudinal updates
Revisit important claims when new episodes arrive, the context changes, or a validation experiment concludes. Preserve prior versions and explain why a claim changed. Personality tendencies may vary by role, stress, culture, and life stage; model scope conditions rather than treating a person as fixed.

## Privacy and consent
Collect only information needed for the stated goal. Sensitive history and third-party feedback require appropriate consent and careful access control. A shareable profile is a separate, minimized projection; never expose raw interview notes by default.
