# Shared Matching Criteria Catalog

This catalog is a starting point, not a mandatory questionnaire. Select only criteria relevant to the decision; let participants add, remove, or reword them before reviewing evidence.

## Common criteria
- Values and principles in practice
- Goals and time horizon
- Communication and decision-making
- Preferred pace, structure, and autonomy
- Risk tolerance and uncertainty
- Location, schedule, and lifestyle constraints
- Resources and responsibilities
- Growth, learning, and adaptability
- Non-negotiables and flexible preferences
- Evidence quality and unresolved assumptions

## Career-specific criteria
- Role outcomes and deliverables
- Essential day-one skills vs trainable skills
- Demonstrated achievements and work samples
- Transferable skills and learning plan
- Manager style, feedback, and decision rights
- Team norms, culture, and collaboration
- Compensation, benefits, stability, and growth
- Workload, schedule, location, accessibility, and travel
- Resources and support needed to succeed
- Employer's evidence about the role and work environment

Use only criteria relevant to performing the job and the candidate's stated preferences. Do not include protected or sensitive traits, family plans, health status, or unrelated personal history.

## Partner / marriage-specific criteria
- Commitment and relationship expectations
- Core values translated into everyday behavior
- Children and parenting, if both choose to discuss
- Family involvement, boundaries, and caregiving
- Money management and financial responsibilities
- Conflict patterns, repair, and communication
- Location, lifestyle, career plans, and time together
- Independence, friendships, and privacy
- Affection, intimacy, and boundaries only by mutual choice
- Deal-breakers, flexibility, and willingness to negotiate

Do not presume that a cultural norm, gender role, or one person's preference is universal. A difference is not automatically a conflict; discuss the practical arrangement each person would accept.

## Importance labels
- **Must-have:** the person says the condition is essential. Do not infer it for them.
- **Important:** materially affects the decision but may allow negotiation.
- **Preference:** useful but flexible.
- **Exploratory:** worth discussing; not yet a requirement.

Importance labels are not numeric weights. Keep each participant's view visible instead of combining them into an opaque score.
