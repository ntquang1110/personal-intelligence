# Interview evidence note — workload and unfinished product formulas

**Artifact ID:** PHM-CASE-WORKLOAD-001  
**Status:** provisional evidence record; not an independent assessment  
**Source class:** direct user self-report in structured interview, rounds 1–3  
**Scope:** understand the user's reported bottleneck in a new pig-health product development project  
**Privacy:** retain only the details needed for the Personal Human Map; do not treat this case as a public biography.

## Evidence ledger

- **PHM-EV-001 (Round 1, project description):** User describes a new product project targeting a pig disease through understanding the pathology and interaction pathways of substances, rather than relying on medication as the primary approach.
- **PHM-EV-002 (Round 1, reported outcome):** User reports one provisional formula has reached a clinical-trial stage and appears effective; two other formulas remain unfinished. No trial protocol, data, safety evidence, or independent review is included in this interview record.
- **PHM-EV-003 (Round 1, self-assessment):** User reports that daily tracking and pushing goals forward are reasonably steady, while time distribution and focus are not yet productive enough.
- **PHM-EV-004 (Round 2, bottleneck selection):** User selects “too many goals relative to available time” as the closest description of the recent bottleneck. No specific work episode or time log is supplied.
- **PHM-EV-005 (Round 3, constraint-choice context):** User reports that some goals are constrained by commitments or dependencies. Which goals, how binding those constraints are, and who controls them are not yet specified.

## What the evidence supports

- The user has a concrete product-development project with one reported preliminary milestone and two unfinished formulas.
- The user perceives a mismatch between active goals and available time.
- At least some goals are reported to be constrained by commitments or dependencies.
- The available evidence is primarily self-report and comes from one interview lineage; repeated discussion in assistant summaries is not independent corroboration.

## What the evidence does not yet support

- That the user is inherently distractible, overcommitted, perfectionistic, undisciplined, or poor at time management.
- That external constraints explain most of the delay, or that personal choices explain most of it.
- That total work demand actually exceeds available capacity; no effort estimates or time budget have been provided.
- That context switching or decision uncertainty is the primary cause.
- Any independent conclusion about formula efficacy, safety, or the user's individual research capability.

## Competing mechanisms to test

### M1 — Capacity overload
**Mechanism:** estimated effort across required and chosen goals exceeds available work capacity.  
**Predictions:** even with protected focus and low interruptions, the total committed workload cannot fit.  
**Weakening evidence:** a realistic effort inventory shows adequate capacity with substantial unused time.

### M2 — External constraint/dependency load
**Mechanism:** deadlines, coordination, waiting for materials/data/trials, or upstream dependencies constrain when work can proceed.  
**Predictions:** delays cluster around documented dependencies or fragmented mandatory commitments.  
**Weakening evidence:** most delay occurs in controllable tasks with all inputs available.

### M3 — Active-goal / work-in-progress overload
**Mechanism:** too many goals are kept active concurrently, increasing coordination and context restoration costs.  
**Predictions:** temporarily limiting active work improves milestone throughput even if total goals remain on the roadmap.  
**Weakening evidence:** few goals are active, context-switching is low, and a WIP limit has no material effect.

### M4 — Discretionary overcommitment
**Mechanism:** goals that could be deferred remain active alongside required commitments.  
**Predictions:** a meaningful share of time goes to goals that can be paused without violating a binding obligation; pausing them improves priority-goal throughput.  
**Weakening evidence:** almost all capacity is consumed by genuinely required work, with little discretionary load.

### M5 — Decision uncertainty
**Mechanism:** unclear decision criteria or unresolved scientific questions delay the next action.  
**Predictions:** work stalls at choices with competing hypotheses or no defined evidence threshold; specifying a decision rule helps.  
**Weakening evidence:** next actions and decision criteria are already clear, but execution is blocked elsewhere.

These mechanisms may coexist. Do not rank them by confidence until evidence distinguishes them.

## Next minimum dataset

For each active goal, collect only what is feasible:
- required / negotiable / discretionary / blocked / unclear;
- who can change the constraint and consequence of delay;
- next concrete milestone and due date;
- rough effort estimate and confidence in that estimate;
- actual active-work time versus waiting time;
- number of simultaneously active goals;
- one recent episode showing what happened during a work session.

Do not invent time estimates. Start with a one-week baseline; only then select a reversible intervention.
